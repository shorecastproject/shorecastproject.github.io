About SHORECAST

For illustrative purposes only. Background photo source: OCNA
Sandy beaches, which covers around a third of the world's ice-free coastline, provide many services and play a crucial role in buffering against flooding. With the rise in sea level due to climate change, they could retreat alarmingly (almost half could be close to extinction by the end of the century). Their evolution is influenced by various complex natural processes that interact over a wide range of spatio-temporal scales, as well as by human factors, such as shoreline management actions. As a consequence, their prediction remains a challenge.

SHORECAST is an ANR-funded project launched on 2 February 2026, developing hybrid approaches to predict the future evolution of sandy coastlines. It combines numerical modeling, data assimilation, metamodeling and reduced-complexity models to address climate change and coastal management challenges across different scales. Coordinated by Déborah Idier (BRGM), the consortium brings together BRGM, ISAE-SUPAERO, IMT, EPOC and WMC (see details).

Key words: coastal flooding, meta-model, local scale, forecast, risk-based, hazard, uncertainty
