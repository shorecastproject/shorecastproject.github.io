About SHORECAST

Sandy beaches, which covers around a third of the world's ice-free coastline, provide many services and play a crucial role in buffering against flooding. With the rise in sea level due to climate change, they could retreat alarmingly (almost half could be close to extinction by the end of the century). Their evolution is influenced by various complex natural processes that interact over a wide range of spatio-temporal scales, as well as by human factors, such as shoreline management actions. As a consequence, their prediction remains a challenge.

SHORECAST aims to create a robust and fast hybrid modeling approach to predict shoreline evolution of sandy beaches, considering climate change and dynamic coastal management strategies across various settings and scales (local to regional, year to century). The key objectives are to:
  1. Develop a metamodeling approach for fast prediction of 2D (in space) wave conditions at breaking from offshore waves and 2D bathymetry
  2. Extend mono-source 1D (in space) data assimilation methods to multi-source (in-situ and satellite) 2D data assimilation in shoreline models, by exploring model parameter dependencies on local and remote conditions
  3. Support the transition from models that neglect coastal management actions to those that include dynamic adaptation measures
  4. Assess how the cascade of uncertainties affect shoreline predictions over time.
